Project Overview

Neurokami is a mobile app concept designed for the neurodiverse community. The project focused on UI/UX design and the consideration of accessibility features, bringing a resource library together with ways to connect and build community.

The Design Process

From research to screen

01 · Research

User research

The project paired user personas with peer-reviewed research on autism, ADHD, and dyslexia to guide accessibility considerations for the app.

ADHD · Student

Marel Brick

22 · Student · Boston, MA

Context

A university student who commutes and balances classes, activities, and volunteering. Marel wants to meet people beyond a small existing friend group.

Challenges

Time management and visual disorganization can make it hard to stay on top of classes and assignments. Notifications and busy interfaces can pull attention away.

Design need: make social connection comfortable and keep key information organized and easy to return to.

ASD · Software Developer

Anna Rose

27 · Software Developer · San Diego, CA

Context

A full-stack developer who enjoys gaming and feels more comfortable socializing online. Anna is interested in forming new relationships.

Challenges

Starting conversations can be difficult. Cluttered visuals, bright colors, sound, unexpected movement, jargon, and sarcasm can make digital interactions harder to parse.

Design need: offer clear, calm screens and predictable ways to discover and join conversations.

Dyslexia · Engineer

Jack Stone

29 · Engineer · Detroit, MI

Context

An engineer with a busy work week who enjoys hands-on projects and is open to meeting people with similar interests.

Challenges

Spelling can make messaging frustrating, and long processes are difficult to stick with. Jack uses spell-check and prefers to keep several tasks moving.

Design need: keep interactions concise, make text comfortable to read, and reduce friction in messaging.

03 · Visual Design & Accessibility

A clear, accessible interface

A soft purple palette, clear typography, rounded controls, and persistent navigation shape the interface, with accessibility options built into settings.

Color palette

The standard interface uses saturated purple and magenta with teal and light neutral tones. Calm Mode has a separate, softer palette.

Primary Purple · #5B46B8

The main action color, used for buttons and prominent interface controls.

Calm Mode palette

An optional softer palette for neurodivergent users who prefer a quieter visual experience.

Muted Purple · #72668A

A softened purple that keeps the brand color present with a gentler feel.

Accessibility options

Neurokami includes settings that let people adjust the interface to suit their preferences.

Typography hierarchy

The exported screens don’t specify a font family, so these specimens describe the visible type roles and hierarchy without guessing the font name.

Display · Large / Bold

Large, bold type gives welcome and screen titles a clear first read.

04 · Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Work out the hierarchy before the visual details.

Low-fidelity screens map the basic layout and navigation for sign-in, the home feed, conversations, and the resource library. Keeping these screens simple makes the content order and core actions easy to assess.
